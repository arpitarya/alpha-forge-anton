**Rule (external):** Every Indian equity round-trip pays a stack of frictions
beyond brokerage: STT on delivery buy and sell, stamp duty on the buy leg,
exchange transaction charges, SEBI turnover fee, and 18% GST on brokerage.
Individually small, together they make a delivery round-trip cost roughly
0.2–0.3% of turnover even on a zero-brokerage plan — before any tax on gains.

Two standing consequences:

- **Churn is a guaranteed negative-sum game.** A strategy must clear the
  friction stack *plus* STCG tax to beat doing nothing. Rebalancing should
  trade as little as the band discipline allows.
- **Recurring costs dominate one-off costs.** A regular mutual-fund plan's
  extra ~0.5–1.0% annual expense ratio versus the direct plan compounds into a
  far larger drag than any transaction charge — always prefer **direct plans**
  and low-TER index funds where the strategy allows.

**Source:** STT — Securities Transaction Tax (Finance Act 2004, as amended);
stamp duty — Indian Stamp Act amendments 2020. Rates change in Finance Acts —
verify before hard-coding any number.

**Applies to:** any trade recommendation or projection. Expected-return
assumptions are gross; advice that triggers trades should acknowledge friction,
and projections over long horizons should use direct-plan/low-TER assumptions.
